## 1. Car facts & trivia

**Origins & purchase**
1. The car is a **1967 Chevrolet Impala Sport Sedan** (4-door hardtop), color black.
2. Per the Super-wiki fan-history timeline, the hero car was built in **Janesville, Wisconsin on 24 April 1967**, and sold new to a first owner for **$3,999** — this specific origin story/date is Super-wiki fan-compiled lore layered onto the show's own "Swan Song" narration (see below), not dialogue from the show itself, so treat the precise date/price as fan-wiki elaboration rather than aired canon.
3. **John Winchester buys the car in 1973** in the episode **S4E3 "In the Beginning."** Time-traveling Dean (posing as "Dean Van Halen") meets his own father at a car lot and talks him out of buying an old Volkswagen van, steering him toward the Impala instead — creating a predestination paradox (Dean's own advice is why John ends up owning the car Dean grew up in). The dealership is **Rainbow Motors** in Lawrence, Kansas. [Source: Super-wiki "In The Beginning"; WebSearch aggregation]
4. Engine, per **Dean's own line** describing the car (episode **S4E3 "In the Beginning"**, spoken to young John Winchester): **"327 four-barrel, 275 horses. A little TLC and this thing is cherry."** This is the in-universe/dialogue spec for the engine — a 327-cubic-inch V8 with a 4-barrel carburetor, 275 hp.
5. Real 1967 Impala factory engine options ran from an inline-six up to big-block 396 and 427 V8s (325–385 hp); the hero picture car(s) used on production reportedly did **not** run a real 327 — see production section below for the actual hardware.
6. **License plates change over the run** (per Super-wiki's compiled plate history):
   - `C-45P4` — 1973, era of John's purchase.
   - `BQN 9R3` — used in 1992 & 1997-set flashback material.
   - **`KAZ 2Y5`** — Kansas plate, 2005–2007 (the plate seen for most of the early seasons); a nod to "Kansas" plus "2005," the show's premiere year.
   - `RMD 5H2` — the "Wishverse" alternate-2006 Kansas plate (from S5's "The French Mistake"/wish-verse-adjacent material — treat as a minor/alt-timeline detail, not fully verified against a primary transcript).
   - **`CNK 80Q3`** — Ohio plate, used from **S2E20 "What Is and What Should Never Be"** onward (some sources tie the swap to the boys becoming fugitives after being arrested by FBI agent Victor Hendricksen in the preceding episode, **S2E19 "Folsom Prison Blues,"** forcing them to ditch the traceable Kansas plate). Off-screen, fans widely read the Ohio plate as a nod to series creator Eric Kripke, who is from Ohio. **This in-universe justification (Hendricksen tracking the plate) is the commonly repeated fan explanation but is somewhat reconstructed after the fact — treat the "why" as fandom consensus rather than an explicit on-screen line, if you can't verify a direct quote.**
7. **Nickname "Metallicar":** this is a **fan-created** nickname, not dialogue from the show. Per Fanlore/Super-wiki-adjacent sources, it originated on the fan forum Television Without Pity, first used by a poster named "Cyb" on 11 October 2005, riffing on Dean's love of Metallica and other metal/classic rock. It spread through fandom from there. No canon episode has Dean react to being told his car is called "Metallicar" — it's fandom's word, not the show's. In-show, Dean just calls her **"Baby."**
8. Dean first calls the car **"Baby"** on-screen in **S2E3 "Bloodlust,"** per fan-wiki episode tagging (some sources cite this as the point the nickname is "dubbed" on screen).

**Damage & rebuilds**
9. **Season 1 finale, "Devil's Trap" (S1E22):** a demon (Azazel) possesses a truck driver and T-bones the Impala with an 18-wheeler as the Winchesters flee — leaving the car (and John, Sam and Dean) badly wrecked as a cliffhanger.
10. **Season 2 premiere, "In My Time of Dying" (S2E1):** the wrecked Impala is towed to **Bobby Singer's** salvage yard; Bobby says it's beyond saving, but Sam insists it be kept for Dean to fix once he recovers.
11. **S2E2 "Everybody Loves a Clown":** Dean is shown actually rebuilding the car from the frame up at Bobby's, using the restoration as a way to process grief over John's death (John died sacrificing himself/the Colt deal to save Dean at the end of S2E1).
12. Per Chuck's later "Swan Song" narration (see quote below), when Dean rebuilt the car "from the ground up" he deliberately preserved the small personal details (the army man, the Legos) rather than cleaning them out — "it's the blemishes that make her beautiful."
13. Further damage across the run, per Super-wiki's damage log (aggregated, not all individually re-verified against transcripts): windshield cracked after Dean's beating by Lucifer (season 5 era); the car is flipped onto its roof with windows blown out (season 6 era); roof straightened and car repainted (season 7); front/rear windows shattered and front end smashed (season 11 era); a hellhound dents a rear quarter panel and crushes the hood/windshield (season 12 era); and in season 15 the car suffers a string of mechanical breakdowns, narratively tied to the Winchesters' "luck" being stripped from them by Chuck/God.
14. **The army man in the ashtray:** Sam, as a kid, crammed a green plastic army man into one of the Impala's ashtrays; it's still stuck there years later. In the series finale run-up (**S5E22 "Swan Song"**), this specific memory — Dean recounting/Sam recalling the army man — is one of the things that helps Sam briefly regain control of his body from Lucifer.
15. **Legos in the vents:** young Dean shoved Lego blocks into the car's heating vents; per Chuck's narration, to this day when the heat comes on, you can hear them rattle. When Dean rebuilt the car after the S1 crash, he made sure to put things like this back / preserve them.
16. **Initials carved:** Sam and Dean carved their initials ("SW" / "DW") into the **wood trim under the rear package tray** (the rear window deck, behind the back seat) as kids. Visible in the "Swan Song" (S5E22) flashback and referenced again in **S11E4 "Baby."** Years later, in the Men of Letters bunker, Dean and Sam carve their initials into the library table too, deliberately echoing the car.
17. **Combination lock on the weapons compartment:** Super-wiki lists the trunk's hidden weapons-compartment combination as **11-02-83** — this is widely cited in fan wikis as Dean's birthdate (born January 24, 1979, per other sources) or otherwise a personal-significance number; treat the exact digits as fan-wiki trivia not independently re-verified here against a clear on-screen shot.
18. The car has a **factory-style bench front seat** with a column-mounted gearshift, and runs a **cassette player** (no CD player — a recurring joke, especially once Sam gets an adapter).
19. Sam adds/rigs an **iPod/aux jack** to the stereo at one point (while Dean is in Hell, S3→S4 transition) — Dean rips it out and goes back to cassettes once he's back topside. (Widely repeated fan-wiki trivia; treat as generally reliable but not individually re-checked against a transcript here.)

**Chuck's "Swan Song" narration (S5E22)** — verbatim, cross-checked against IMDb/quote aggregators/Super-wiki transcript:
> "On April 21, 1967, the 100 millionth GM vehicle rolled off the line at the plant in Janesville — a blue two-door Caprice. There was a big ceremony, speeches. The lieutenant governor even showed up. Three days later, another car rolled off that same line. No one gave two craps about her. But they should have, because this 1967 Chevrolet Impala would turn out to be the most important car — no, the most important object — in pretty much the whole universe."
>
> "The Impala, of course, has all the things other cars have. And a few things they don't. But none of that stuff is important. This is the stuff that's important: the army man that Sam crammed into the ashtray, it's still stuck there... The Legos that Dean shoved into the vents — to this day, heat comes on, they can hear them rattle. These are the things that make the car theirs, really theirs. Even when Dean rebuilt it from the ground up, he made sure all these little things stayed. 'Cause it's the blemishes that make her beautiful."

**"Driver picks the music" and cassette culture**
20. The rule is established in the **Pilot (S1E1)**. Full exchange (verified via quotes.net / Wikiquote):
    > Sam: "I swear, man, you gotta update your cassette tape collection." Dean: "Why?" Sam: "Well, for one, they're cassette tapes. And two: Black Sabbath, Motörhead, Metallica?! It's the greatest hits of mullet rock." Dean: "House rules, Sammy. Driver picks the music, shotgun shuts his cake hole."
21. This "driver picks the music" rule recurs as a running gag/callback across the whole series (e.g. referenced again with Jack and with Sam driving in later seasons).

**S11E4 "Baby" — the car's-POV episode**
22. **S11E4, titled "Baby,"** is shot almost entirely from inside/around the Impala — no scene takes place outside the car's presence. It opens with Dean washing the Impala in the **Men of Letters bunker's underground garage**, Sam joining him, before they head out on the road; the unusual bottle-episode structure was a deliberate stylistic choice to center the car itself as a character. [Source: Super-wiki 11.04 Baby; TV Tropes recap; WFB "Deeper Look" essay]
23. The bunker's private underground garage (introduced with the Men of Letters bunker itself in **S8**) becomes Baby's home base/parking spot for the remainder of the series once the brothers move in.

**Season 7 / Leviathan-era storage**
24. In **season 7**, Leviathans (shapeshifted to look like Sam and Dean) go on a killing spree while driving a duplicate '67 Impala, making the real Sam and Dean wanted fugitives. On hacker/ally **Frank Devereaux's** advice, the brothers put the real Impala into **long-term storage** to avoid detection — she's largely absent for a stretch of season 7. In her place the show has the boys drive a rotating cast of other cars, including (per aggregated fan-wiki/episode trivia): a 1986 Pontiac Acadian (**S7E6 "Slash Fiction"**), a stolen 1974 Dodge Challenger (**S7E7 "The Mentalists"**), and a 1969 Buick Special (**S7E11 "Adventures in Babysitting"**).
25. Dean's memorable line on getting the Impala back out of storage, **"Nobody puts Baby in a corner!"**, is from **S7E6 "Slash Fiction."**

**Production: how many cars, who built/kept them**
26. **Sources disagree significantly on the exact number of Impalas used across the show's 15 seasons** — figures found range from **3 "Hero" cars** up to **"at least nine."** The most detailed breakdown found (Super-wiki, corroborated loosely by a Hotcars retrospective) describes roughly: multiple **"Hero"** cars (camera-ready, best cosmetic condition), several **"Stunt"** cars (built to take abuse), a couple of wrecked/destroyed cars, and at least one **cut-up car** with removable roof/doors for interior camera rigging. **Treat the precise count as unresolved/disputed** rather than a single hard number — flag this explicitly if quoting a figure on the fan site.
27. The **hero car** is reported (Hotcars) to have run a **GM 502-cubic-inch big-block crate engine** for performance filming — a big step up from the fictional "327" Dean quotes in dialogue — while most of the other production cars used more conventional, less powerful stock-type drivetrains.
28. **Jensen Ackles (Dean)** kept one of the production Impalas after the show wrapped in 2020; multiple sources describe this as written into his **Season 15 contract**. His car reportedly needed **factory air-conditioning added** before he took it home to Texas (accounts differ on whether his car had the big 502 or a 283 small-block — sources conflict here too).
29. Some sources also mention **Jared Padalecki (Sam)** taking home a second production car (nicknamed informally "Lil' Bro" in at least one fan-wiki summary) — **this specific claim was harder to independently corroborate across multiple outlets and should be treated as less certain than the Ackles car-keeping story**, which is repeated more consistently (e.g. ScreenRant's "Jensen Ackles keeping Dean's Impala is in his Season 15 contract").
30. Other production cars reportedly ended up at the **Petersen Automotive Museum** and the **Warner Bros. Picture Car Vault**; some fan-wiki summaries claim remaining cars were destroyed per studio policy — **not independently verified here, treat as unconfirmed fan-wiki claim.**
31. **Why a 1967 Impala at all:** creator **Eric Kripke** originally wanted the "hero car" to be a mid-1960s **Ford Mustang** (some sources say specifically a '65 Mustang), envisioning the car as a recurring character similar to K.I.T.T. in *Knight Rider*. A neighbor of his in Venice, California — a mechanic — reportedly talked him out of it. Kripke's own account (as quoted by TV Insider/TVLine): *"When I told my neighbor in Venice, who was a mechanic, that I wanted a badass car and was thinking of a '65 Mustang, he said, 'Yeah, a Mustang is the perfect car if you're a p***y,' and without missing a beat, he said, 'You want a '67 Impala because you can put a body in that trunk.'"* Kripke looked it up and decided it was perfect — partly for the intimidating look, and partly because the huge trunk could plausibly hide a hunter's weapons arsenal (and, as the joke went, a body).
32. Door-squeak sound design, side spotlights (removed for camera rigging in season 3, restored by fan campaign by around season 14), and a custom trailer hitch added for a season 14 episode are all cited (Super-wiki) as small but deliberate continuity/practical details the crew tracked across 15 seasons.

**The finale — S15E20 "Carry On"**
33. In the series finale, **Dean dies (fatally wounded by a vampire-impaled rebar in a hunt, in the preceding episode "Inherit the Earth")** and goes to Heaven, where his own personal Heaven includes the classic road, a burger joint, and — centrally — **the Impala.** He gets in and drives, with **"Carry On Wayward Son" by Kansas** playing on the radio; Dean is shown loving the song as it plays. Driving a winding road through a beautiful landscape, he eventually stops on a bridge, gets out, and is reunited with **Sam**, who has just arrived in Heaven decades later (after living out a full human life on Earth, marrying, raising a son he named Dean). The brothers embrace as the show ends. This positions the Impala as literally part of Dean's idea of Heaven/paradise.

## 3. Devil's Trap lore

- **What it does:** A Devil's Trap is a five-pointed-star sigil (traditionally enclosed in one or two circles, with additional symbolic flourishes) that, once a demon is standing inside it, **completely traps the demon** — it cannot possess a new host, cannot use most of its powers to escape, and cannot physically leave the boundary of the trap.
- **How it can be broken:** the trap only works if its lines are **unbroken**. If any part of the drawn/carved/painted symbol is smudged, scratched out, or otherwise interrupted, the trap fails and the demon is freed. Demons trapped inside sometimes strain hard enough to cause secondary effects (tremors, minor property damage) but generally cannot break the trap directly themselves — the classic escape route in the show is a third party (deliberately or accidentally) breaking the line.
- **How it can be drawn:** the show establishes it can be rendered in blood, chalk, paint, marker, carved into a surface, or even improvised from materials like ash, iron, or chains laid out in the trap's shape — the material doesn't matter, only the correct unbroken geometry.
- **Introducing episode:** the concept and the season's central demon-trap plotline are the backbone of the **season 1 finale, "Devil's Trap" (S1E22)** — the episode is literally named after the symbol, built around Meg and Azazel's confrontation with the Winchesters while the Colt (and a trap protecting it) are central to the plot.
- **The trunk-lid trap:** the Winchesters (Sam, in at least one on-screen instance) paint/draw a Devil's Trap on the **underside of the Impala's trunk lid**, so that if a demon reaches into the trunk (e.g. to grab the Colt), it gets caught the moment it looks/reaches in. This is one of the more iconic small pieces of the car's "weaponization" and ties directly to protecting the Colt specifically.
- **Anti-possession tattoo — related but distinct symbol:** the anti-possession tattoo (a pentagram/sigil the brothers get inked onto their chests) works on a related principle but for a different purpose — it prevents a demon from **possessing the wearer at all**, rather than trapping a demon that's already manifested/embodied. Per aggregated fan-wiki sourcing, the tattoo's introduction traces to **season 3, episode 12, "Jus in Bello,"** after Sam is possessed by the demon Meg — Bobby Singer provides the brothers with the protective charm/symbol that becomes their tattoos. **This means the anti-possession tattoo postdates the Devil's Trap symbol's introduction by about two seasons — they are thematically related (both demon-based protective sigils) but not introduced in the same episode.** If it's damaged/burned off, the tattoo's protection is negated.

14. **S15E20 "Carry On" (series finale):** *"Hey, Baby."* — Dean, greeting the Impala as he arrives in his personal Heaven, just before his final drive and reunion with Sam.
