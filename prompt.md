# Baby Impala

IMPORTANT: This is a new experiment, starting from scratch. Do not look at other projects, memories, worklogs, todos. Do not copy anything from other projects on this machine.

I've been re-watching Supernatural lately and I want to make a nice/fun fan page centered on Dean's car "Baby".
A 3d hyper realistic representation of "Baby" the 1967 Chevrolet Impala, with all the distinctive features from the show and the lore around it, classic rock music, and an exploded view animation.

## About "Baby"

The Winchester brothers’ car is a black 1967 Chevrolet Impala four-door hardtop, although it’s much more specifically Dean’s car. He calls her “Baby”.

### The car itself
In the show, Dean describes it as having a 327 cu in (5.4 L) V8, four-barrel carburetor and 275 hp.

### The trunk
The trunk is essentially the Winchesters' mobile armory. Beneath the false floor they carry shotguns, handguns, machetes, knives, stakes, silver weapons, ammunition, holy water, salt and whatever specialized equipment a particular hunt requires.

The trunk is one of Baby's most distinctive Supernatural-specific features. It's basically a concealed mobile hunter's armory, and the demon warding is built directly into it.

#### The demon warding

When Dean opens the trunk, the underside of the trunk lid has a large Devil's Trap painted on it (see `references/trunk-devils-trap.webp`, `open-trunk.jpg`, `inside-trunk-tools.jpg`).

It's the familiar Supernatural design: a large pentagram enclosed within circular rings, with occult markings/sigils in and around it, plus smaller separate sigils to its left and right. It's hand-painted in rough off-white/cream strokes on the black underside of the lid.

In Supernatural lore, a Devil's Trap has a very specific function: a demon that enters its boundary becomes trapped and can't leave until the symbol is damaged or broken. It also prevents the demon from using some of its abilities to escape.

The clever part of Baby's installation is its location. When the trunk lid is closed, the Devil's Trap sits directly over the contents of the trunk. So if something demonic is being transported in the trunk, the Impala can function as a kind of mobile demon containment cell.

It's not merely decorative hunter iconography.

#### Under the false floor

The false floor is a hinged panel that lifts up and stands like a pegboard (see `references/inside-trunk-tools.jpg`). Its underside is lined with black felt, and weapons are mounted on it in straps and holsters: knives, a machete, stakes, a hatchet, an arrow, a cross, a sage bundle, brass knuckles, a dreamcatcher and more. Below it, a gray-carpeted tray holds the rest of the gear: shotguns, ammo boxes and cans, a bandolier of shells, flasks, jars, flashlights, fake IDs and papers. The exact inventory changes throughout the series, but you regularly see things along the lines of:

Firearms: several shotguns, rifles and handguns, including weapons loaded with specialized ammunition such as rock salt or silver bullets.

Bladed weapons: machetes, large knives and various specialized blades used against particular creatures.

Anti-supernatural supplies: containers of salt, holy water, wooden stakes, silver implements and other creature-specific materials.

Ammunition: boxes and loose ammunition for the different guns, including the specialized rounds the brothers manufacture themselves.

Hunter equipment: flashlights, ropes/chains, tools and assorted equipment needed to investigate or restrain supernatural creatures.

There's also room for whatever specialized weapon the current hunt requires. The trunk essentially functions as the Winchesters' equipment inventory between episodes.

### How Dean got it

The car originally belonged to John Winchester, Dean and Sam's father.

There's a fun time-travel loop involved. In S4E3, “In the Beginning,” Dean travels back to 1973 and encounters young John. John is considering buying a VW van, and Dean himself convinces him to buy the Impala instead, praising the 327 V8. So Dean indirectly causes his father to buy the car that will eventually be passed down to him.

That makes Baby essentially a Winchester family heirloom.

### The license plate

The original Kansas plate is:

KAZ 2Y5

It's an Easter egg: KAZ → Kansas, while 2Y5 → 2005, the year Supernatural premiered.

Later, when Sam and Dean are wanted by the FBI, Dean changes it to an Ohio plate:

CNK 80Q3

That becomes the familiar plate for much of the rest of the series.

### Why Baby matters so much

The Impala isn't merely Dean's favorite possession. It's effectively the brothers' permanent home.

The Season 5 finale “Swan Song” makes this explicit. We learn about tiny remnants of their childhood still inside the car—an army man Sam jammed into the ashtray, Lego pieces Dean put into the vents, and their initials carved into the car. Those seemingly insignificant details ultimately become important to the story.

The idea is that although Sam and Dean spend their lives in motels and rarely have a conventional home, Baby is the one home that travels with them.

And Dean repeatedly rebuilds her after severe damage. Most famously, she's essentially destroyed after the truck crash at the end of Season 1, and Dean reconstructs her.

There's an entire episode from the car's perspective

One of the show's most unusual episodes is S11E4, “Baby.”

Almost the entire story is presented from cameras positioned in or around the Impala. Instead of following Sam and Dean normally, you're effectively sitting in Baby while their lives happen around you.

### Distinctive features

Gloss black 1967 Chevrolet Impala 4-door hardtop — importantly, it's the four-door pillarless hardtop, not the much more commonly collected two-door Impala.
Chrome-heavy exterior — chrome bumpers, window surrounds, door handles and the long factory side/body trim.
Distinctive wheels — 15" polished chrome aftermarket wheels (a smooth dished face with a ring of small round holes around the center and a chrome center cap), with fairly chunky black tires. See `references/wheels.jpg`. They're one of the easiest ways to distinguish Baby from a stock-looking '67 Impala.
License plates — early on, the iconic Kansas KAZ 2Y5 plate; later, most commonly the Ohio CNK 80Q3 plate.
Two spotlights by the A-pillars/windshield — small round chrome Unity-style spotlights, one on each side. These are quite visually distinctive.
Long radio antenna on the front passenger-side fender.
Mirrors — a single round chrome side mirror on the driver's door, with a tiny Chevy logo on it. There's no passenger-side mirror. The interior rear-view mirror is missing in some seasons.
Badges — minimal. The only ones are the "Chevrolet" script on the grille and a tiny Chevy bowtie on the chrome strip between the taillights. There are no logos on the sides or the rest of the rear.
Front — quad round headlights, a horizontal-bar chrome grille, a V-shaped hood and nose, corner lights, and vertical chrome bumper guards on the front and rear bumpers.
Rear — triple-segment taillights (red, clear, red) set in chrome surrounds, a trunk lock in the center of the lid, and dual exhaust.
Two-tone interior — black vinyl bench seats (front and back, with vertical pleats) and black carpet, but a tan/beige padded dash, tan door panels and a tan headliner. See `references/interior.jpg`, `backseat.jpg`, `steering-wheel.jpg`.
Steering wheel — a large, thin, 3-spoke tan wheel with a chrome horn button, wrapped in black perforated leather.
Tape deck — an aftermarket slot-loading cassette deck in the dash: an FM/AM dial window on top, a horizontal cassette slot below, and chunky olive/khaki push buttons. See `references/radio.jpg`.
Hidden weapons compartment — the trunk has a custom false floor that lifts up like a pegboard to reveal the Winchester arsenal, with the Devil's Trap on the underside of the trunk lid above it. It's arguably Baby's most distinctive modification, although obviously invisible from outside.
Little bits of Winchester history — Sam's green toy soldier stuck in the chrome ashtray on the rear door panel (`references/toy-soldier.jpg`), Lego pieces pushed into the defroster vent on top of the dash (`references/legos.jpg`), and “S.W.” and “D.W.” carved into the car. These are emphasized in Swan Song as evidence that the Impala has essentially been their home.

One subtle thing Baby doesn't have is equally important: there are no flames, racing stripes, decals or obvious supernatural symbols on the exterior. From a distance she's basically an immaculate, slightly menacing black American sedan.

### Inside Baby

Baby's interior is as recognizable as her exterior. It's two-tone: black vinyl bench seats and black carpet against a tan dash, tan door panels and a tan headliner. The large front bench seat gives the car its classic late-'60s American feel and is the setting for countless conversations between Sam and Dean on the road.

Music is also inseparable from the car. Dean keeps a collection of cassette tapes and almost exclusively plays classic rock. From the Pilot onward, the Impala, its tape deck and Dean's music become part of the show's identity, with bands such as Metallica and Motörhead appearing in his collection.

The interior also carries small remnants of Sam and Dean's childhood. Their initials, D.W. and S.W., are carved into the car, Sam left a little green army man wedged in the ashtray, and Dean stuffed Lego pieces into the defroster vent on the dash.

These details become important in the Season 5 finale, “Swan Song.” When Lucifer is controlling Sam, seeing the army man inside Baby triggers memories of growing up with Dean and helps Sam regain control. The tiny imperfections turn out to be much more important than they initially appear.

## The Website

The car is the main focus of the website, and the user can interract with it to explore trivia and distinctive features.
The website should work on both desktop and mobile.

### The Scene

The scene should be centered around the "Baby Impala". The user should be able to rotate around it, zoom in, click and interract with it.
The user should be able to see details and trivia about the distinctive features and the car itself.

### Look & Feel

**Focus:** Baby is the hero. Keep the camera close, with the car always at the center of the view. No landscapes, no wide establishing shots, no sky or moon. Nothing should pull attention away from Baby.

**Mood:** Cinematic and moody, with low-key night lighting. Baby is glossy black and slightly menacing, not showroom-shiny.

**Setting (toggleable):**
- *Minimal (default):* just the car on a round patch of wet asphalt, with puddles reflecting the paint and chrome. The patch fades into darkness at its edges.
- *Motel:* adds a run-down roadside motel backdrop close behind the car, with a buzzing, flickering red neon "VACANCY" sign whose light reflects in the puddles, paint and chrome.

**Materials:** Realistic, physically based materials. Deep clear-coat black paint that reflects its surroundings. Mirror chrome that picks up the neon and the lights. Worn black vinyl on the bench seats, against a tan padded dash and tan door panels. Faint dust and road grime on the lower panels. Lived-in, not museum-new.

**Atmosphere (keep it light):** Very light fog low to the ground, dust drifting in the headlight beams, and water droplets on the windshield to match the wet asphalt. Subtle touches only. Don't overwhelm the scene.

**Rain:** The asphalt is always wet and the puddles always stay; they never dry out. The rain toggle only adds or removes falling rain and the ripples it makes in the puddles.

**Lighting moments:** When the engine "starts", the headlights and the dash light up with a warm glow. The two A-pillar spotlights can be switched on and swept through the dark.

**Loading screen:** A journal-style loading screen shows progress while the assets load. When loading finishes, a "Start the engine" button appears. Clicking it plays the engine rumble, turns on the headlights and starts the music. It also counts as the user interaction browsers need before they'll play audio.

**Camera:** After the engine starts, the page opens on a slow cinematic reveal, with headlights cutting through the low fog, before the user gets control. The camera stays low to the ground, the way the show films her.

**UI style:** Worn and analog, like John Winchester's hunter journal: aged paper cards, handwritten notes, typewriter text and torn tape. No glossy modern UI.

**Typography:** A distressed typewriter or serif font for trivia and lore text. A bold condensed font for headings, like the show's title card.

**Sound effects:** An engine rumble on start and a creak when the trunk opens. No ambient sounds like crickets or wind, since the rock music fills that space.

### Trunk View

A dedicated "Trunk view" mode:
1. The camera glides to the back of the car and the trunk lid pops open, revealing the Devil's Trap painted on its underside. The trap has its own lore card.
2. The false floor lifts and stands up like a pegboard, showing the weapons mounted on its felt-lined underside.
3. The gear in the tray below is laid out and fully visible.

The user can zoom in on the trunk and hover over or click each item. Hovering shows the item's name. Clicking opens a journal-style lore page explaining what it's used for, for example:
- silver bullets for werewolves and shapeshifters
- salt and rock-salt shotgun shells for ghosts
- holy water for demons
- iron for spirits and fairies
- machetes for vampires
- wooden stakes, the Colt, and so on

Research the full inventory and the lore behind each item.

### Controls & HUD

All controls share the worn, analog journal style and stay minimal, so the car remains the hero.

1. **View switcher:** Normal / Exploded / Trunk / Interior, styled like tabs on a journal page. The camera glides smoothly between views. Interior puts the camera inside, looking at the bench seat, the dash and the childhood details.
2. **Scene toggles:** Motel on/off, rain on/off, spotlights on/off, engine start.
3. **Plate flip button:** KAZ 2Y5 ↔ CNK 80Q3, for users who don't discover clicking the plate on the car.
4. **Hotspot markers:** small pulsing sigils on the car's distinctive features, with a toggle to hide them.
5. **Lore panel:** a journal page that slides in from the side when a feature or item is clicked. It closes with Esc or a click outside, and becomes a bottom sheet on mobile.
6. **Camera reset:** snaps back to the hero shot.
7. **Audio:** mute and volume controls.
8. **Hint:** a short "drag to rotate · scroll to zoom" hint (touch equivalents on mobile) that fades after first use.

### Animations
There should be an expanded/exploded view where the car is decomposed in individual parts: mid/large size components like engine, wheels, rims, windscreen, doors, trunk lid and more, but not down to nuts and bolts.
When switching between normal and exploded view the parts animate smoothly. They separate slowly and feel heavy, like a blueprint or workshop manual, with dotted guide lines and small handwritten part labels.

The user should be able to interract with the car, and we should have nice animations (for example on click the number plate rotates and switches KAZ 2Y5 -> CNK 80Q3, and back on next click)

### Libraries and Assets

The website should use Threejs or WebGL and no links to external resources/assets (the only exception is music, see below).
It is ok to download public/free assets from online, or build you own, but these should be copied inside the repo so we don't have the risk that they expire/don't work. 
You can also create resources via the Blender mcp if you need to.

**The car model:** Search online for a free, legally usable model of a 1967 Chevrolet Impala **4-door hardtop**, or build or adapt one in Blender. It must be identical to Baby as she appears in the show, matching the reference images and the distinctive features listed above. A 2-door Impala, or a generic '60s sedan, is not acceptable.

**Performance:** Target ~60 fps on desktop and ~30 fps on mobile. Detect weak devices and lower the quality automatically (shadows, reflections, particles, pixel ratio) so the frame rate holds.

**Reduced motion:** Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Skip the cinematic intro, make camera moves instant or very short, and tone down particles and flicker.

### Music

If possible the user should be able to change "casettes" and tracks to play music from the tv series soundtrack, what Dean would play. For this it is allowed to use external assets: streams, radio, spotify player, soundcloud, or downloaded music - whatever you can use legally.

The music must be legal. You decide the mix: for example, embed Spotify (search for existing playlists of the show's soundtrack) and/or download royalty-free tracks into the repo. Ideally play the actual songs from the show. Otherwise pick tracks in the same style: '70s and '80s classic rock and hard rock, the kind Dean plays.

The cassette player must always be reachable from the UI. The user shouldn't have to reach into the car to press play. It's a skeuomorphic, compact version of Baby's real slot-loading dash deck (`references/radio.jpg`): an FM/AM dial window, a horizontal cassette slot with the hand-labeled edge of the tape visible, and chunky olive/khaki push buttons that press in. It shows the current cassette and song name, with play/pause, prev/next, and a button to switch cassettes.
